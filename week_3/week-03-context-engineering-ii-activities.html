    Activity 1 · Warm-up Concept Quiz

    10 minIndividualFormative

    1. Why not just always send the full conversation history, given large context windows?

    Correct answer: A. Bigger context windows don't remove the cost/latency/quality tradeoffs.

    2. What's the main downside of a pure sliding-window strategy?

    Correct answer: C. Cheap and simple, but nothing before the window is recoverable.

    3. Cosine similarity close to 1.0 between two text embeddings means...

    Correct answer: B. Similarity is about meaning direction in vector space, not literal word overlap.

    4. What does "embed once, store vectors, embed the query at search time" describe?

    Correct answer: A. This is exactly what we scale up into a real RAG pipeline next week.

    Activity 2 · Instructor-Led Live Coding

    45 minWhole group

    Follow along in week-03-context-engineering-ii.ipynb, sections 1–3: build a 10-turn synthetic conversation, apply sliding window vs. rolling summarization side by side, then generate embeddings and compute similarity between related and unrelated sentence pairs.

    Activity 3 · Group Workshop: Design a Memory Strategy

    45 minGroups of 3–4

    Each group is assigned a different scenario: (a) a 5-minute customer support chat, (b) a semester-long tutoring assistant, (c) a code-review assistant working through a huge pull request, (d) a daily journaling companion.

    1. Decide: sliding window, summarization, structured memory extraction, or a combination? Justify with the scenario's actual constraints (how long does context matter? how much does a mistake cost?).
    2. Sketch what specifically gets kept vs. dropped vs. summarized.
    3. Present your design and defend it against one "what if" challenge from another group.
